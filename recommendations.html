Four moves to fix what holds thrift shoppers back: sizing, trust, community and styling.

Chapter 04 / 04
01

Size-inclusive racks

Theory Extended self · Perceived risk

Studies prove that limited sizing and fit options are the major barriers preventing consumers from purchasing used clothes (Belk, 1988; Hur, 2020; Koay et al., 2022). In Malaysia, this gap is widened because Jalan Jalan Japan relies heavily on imported Japanese sizing, which runs smaller, while REFASH and 2nd STREET normally stock up on the standard S-L clothing. Disorganized racks and supply bottlenecks also exclude plus-size shoppers from sustainable thrifting (Dsouza, 2022). To address this, retailers should introduce dedicated sections for Plus Size (XL-4XL), Petite, and Modest-Wear racks in physical outlets as well as additional filters online. This would create strong emotional trust and loyalty for minority customer groups.

02

A 4-tier hangtag grading system

Large outlets such as Jalan Jalan Japan may be overwhelming for first-time customers with unsorted bulk racks, whereas 2nd STREET and REFASH are often criticized for lack of cleanliness and authenticity. For this, there should be a standardized 4-tier classification of items using a hangtag system of (1) Grade A+: New with Tags, (2) Grade A: Professionally Sanitized/Almost New, (3) Grade B: Sanitized/With Minor Signs of Wear, and (4) Grade C: Upcycle-Ready, indicating the actual sanitization process used (such as high temperature steam or ozone) to reduce perceived contamination risk and establish clearer pricing tiers.

03

Campus swaps & mending workshops

To support youth-centered activations, thrift stores can work together with university student unions and green clubs to organize Clothing Swap & Visible Mending Workshops once a month in campus facilities. Students will bring clean and unworn clothing in exchange for cashless tokens that can be used for swaps, encouraging green habits and peer influence between Gen Z consumers.

04

Styled outfit racks

Lastly, to help shoppers visualize how thrift pieces might fit their wardrobe, stores should curate small, featured racks in-store where staff group second-hand clothes into ready-to-wear outfits, such as the “Smart Casual Office” and “Y2K Streetwear”, presented on styled mannequins. Positioning individual thrifted items as cohesive, fashion-forward outfits lowers styling uncertainty and appeals to fashion enthusiasts (Das et al., 2025).
